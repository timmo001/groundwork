---
title: Motion
description: How things move from one place to another, how long it takes and how it eases, and how it turns to a crossfade under reduced motion.
---

Motion shows where something went. When a thing changes place, size or container, it travels there, so the eye follows it; when it jumps, the person has to find it again. Used too much, motion gets in the way, so it's kept short, runs on the tokens below, answers what a person does, and keeps still for anyone who asks for less of it.

## Tokens

| Token | Value | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--gw-motion-instant` | 75ms | A press or a hover |
| `--gw-motion-fast` | 150ms | A color change, or a small move inside one control, such as a switch's knob |
| `--gw-motion-normal` | 250ms | Something traveling inside its container, such as a toggle's pill, or a popover or menu appearing by its button |
| `--gw-motion-slow` | 350ms | Something crossing the view, such as a sheet sliding in, or a tile becoming the whole view |
| `--gw-motion-easing` | `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)` | Moving between two places on screen |
| `--gw-motion-easing-enter` | `cubic-bezier(0.05, 0.7, 0.1, 1)` | Arriving from an edge or out of an anchor: quick, then slowing into place |
| `--gw-motion-easing-exit` | `cubic-bezier(0.3, 0, 0.8, 0.15)` | Leaving: easing off, then speeding away |
| `--gw-motion-delay` | 500ms | How long a loading placeholder waits before it fades in |
| `--gw-motion-fade` | 150ms | A crossfade in place, and what travel turns into under reduced motion |

Pick the duration by how far a thing travels, not by how important it is: the further it goes, the longer it takes, up to `--gw-motion-slow`. Leaving takes one step less than arriving, as nobody needs to watch something go: a sheet slides in on `slow` and out on `normal`.

## Reduced motion

Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, nothing moves, grows or shrinks, but a change that replaces what's on screen still crossfades, so it's seen:

- **Every duration but the fade drops to 1ms**, so anything timed with them happens at once. A control's own change sits under the press that made it, so it's seen without motion.
- **A change across the view crossfades in place** on `--gw-motion-fade`: [`withViewTransition()`](#across-the-view) keeps everything still and fades the old view out and the new one in, where it would have traveled or slid. A bar that leaves by itself, such as [controls over a picture](/behavior#controls-over-a-picture), fades rather than slides.
- **An animation the tokens don't time**, such as a loop, needs its own `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` rule, and turns into a still state there, such as a dim in place of a pulse, so what it showed is still there.
- **A drag still follows the finger.** It's the person moving it, not an animation.

Opacity isn't motion: WCAG's Animation from Interactions counts what changes an element's size, shape or position, and Apple's guidance for Reduce Motion replaces sliding transitions with fades rather than dropping them. A view that changed at once with nothing to show it is easy to miss. Set `--gw-motion-fade` to `1ms` for no fade at all.

## Things travel, they don't jump

- **Changing place, size or container moves there.** A tile that becomes the only picture grows out of its place in the grid, and shrinks back into it when it's done. A picked pill slides to the new option.
- **Arriving comes from somewhere, and leaving goes back there.** A popover or menu grows out of its button, a sheet slides in from its own edge, and a panel or bar docked to a side slides in from that side. Each leaves the way it came.
- **A list makes room and closes its gaps.** An item that arrives comes from where it belongs, such as a notice from its corner, or fades in at its place in a list where nothing else moves; the items around it slide to make room, and slide together when one leaves.
- **Fading is for content arriving in place**, where nothing moves: a page once it has loaded, a panel's body, a list's results. Wrap it in [`gw-fade-in`](/components/feedback#fading-in). A loading placeholder fades in too, after `--gw-motion-delay`, so a quick load never flashes one. Skeletons, spinners and the rings on loading buttons and menu buttons keep their space while they wait, so nothing around them moves; set the token to `0ms` on an element to show its placeholder at once. Switching theme or mode crossfades the page.
- **Motion answers a person's action, never a resize.** A change someone makes, such as opening a bar's other rows or turning on a control that adds one, travels. A change from resizing the window or turning the device doesn't: the person is already watching the size change, and motion would trail behind their drag. A layout that fits itself to its width, such as a [control bar](/components/actions#control-bar) folding controls into More, or a [row](/components/layout#stack-row-and-grid) dropping its labels, snaps to its new shape.
- **One press, one motion.** Two unrelated things never animate for the same press.
- **A second press never waits.** A press during a motion starts the next one straight away, from where things are: a CSS transition turns around mid-way, and a new view transition skips the running one to its end and starts from there. Nobody waits for an animation to finish before they can act.
- **A drag follows the finger.** Something dragged, such as a bar swiped away, moves with the finger with nothing easing it, then on release settles where it's going on `--gw-motion-easing`, or springs back. A release that's past half way, or that's moving fast, about 500px a second, goes on; otherwise it goes back.
- **Only when it can be seen.** Nothing animates while the page is hidden.

## Inside a component

Inside a component, motion is a CSS transition on the tokens: a toggle's pill slides to the new option, a switch's knob glides, menus, popovers and dialogs ease in from `@starting-style`, and notices fade in their corner. A press shows within `--gw-motion-instant`, before the action answers. Every Groundwork component does this already, and an app's own element follows the same table.

## Across the view

A change that moves things across the page, such as a tile becoming the whole view, a panel docking at the side or a page changing, goes through the View Transitions API with `withViewTransition()`. It runs the change straight away where the browser has no View Transitions and while the page is hidden.

Give each element that travels a `view-transition-name` of its own, and the browser moves and resizes it from its old place to its new one. `transitionName()` builds a valid name from an app's own ids:

```ts
import { transitionName, withViewTransition } from "@timmo001/groundwork-ui";

tile.style.viewTransitionName = transitionName("camera", camera.id);

withViewTransition(() => {
  grid.dataset.solo = camera.id;
});
```

Something that only arrives or leaves, rather than moving, slides in from its edge. Groundwork's View Transition classes time and ease both with the tokens; `withViewTransition()` adds them to the page:

| `view-transition-class` | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `gw-travel` | Moves and resizes to its new place on `--gw-motion-slow` and `--gw-motion-easing`, such as a tile becoming the whole view |
| `gw-from-bottom`, `gw-from-top`, `gw-from-start`, `gw-from-end` | Arriving, slides in from that edge on `--gw-motion-slow` and `--gw-motion-easing-enter`; leaving, slides back out a step quicker, on `--gw-motion-normal` and `--gw-motion-easing-exit`. Start and end follow the page's direction |
| `gw-page` | A page's content swapping, on `--gw-motion-slow` and `--gw-motion-easing`. With the `gw-forward` or `gw-back` type, a sibling page or tab: the old slides 30px toward the start (or the end, going back) and fades out over the first 35%, and the new slides in the same way from the other side and fades in over the rest. With neither, a page that isn't related fades through: the old fades out over the first 35%, then the new fades in while growing from 92% |

```css
.dock {
  view-transition-name: dock;
  view-transition-class: gw-from-end;
}

.tile {
  view-transition-class: gw-travel;
}
```

Leave the timing to these classes rather than setting one duration on every group with `::view-transition-group(*)`: that would also time slides, page swaps and the root's crossfade the same, against the rule that the further something goes, the longer it takes.

### Pages and tabs

Moving between pages or tabs changes the content, and the app bar and anything else they share stays still:

- **Siblings slide along their line.** Pages at the same level, such as Live, Recordings and Settings, or the tabs of one view, slide a short way toward the end going forward (to a page further along) and toward the start going back, with a fade between them.
- **Unrelated pages fade through.** A page that isn't next to the last, such as one opened from a search, fades the old out, then the new in.

Give the content a name and the `gw-page` class, and pass the direction as a type:

```css
main {
  view-transition-name: page;
  view-transition-class: gw-page;
}
```

```ts
const go = (to: number) =>
  withViewTransition(() => show(to), {
    types: [to > current ? "gw-forward" : "gw-back"],
  });
```

Types need a recent browser; where it has View Transitions but no types, the page fades through. Groundwork's own controls don't change pages themselves, so the app runs this from its router or from a [toggle](/components/actions#toggle)'s `change`.

A site of separate pages with no client router of its own gets the same with one rule. The browser runs a crossfade between same-origin pages, and `gw-page` works there too once the content has its name and class. A site whose router already runs view transitions, such as Astro's `ClientRouter`, leaves it out, or each page would animate twice:

```css
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference) {
  @view-transition {
    navigation: auto;
  }
}
```

`viewTransitionStyles` is the stylesheet itself, for a page that starts its own transitions or a site of separate pages. It sits in the components layer, so an app's own `::view-transition-*` rules win.

Keep them simple: every name is used once on a page at a time, and they work at document level, never inside a shadow root. Start a second one whenever it's asked for; it doesn't wait for the first.
