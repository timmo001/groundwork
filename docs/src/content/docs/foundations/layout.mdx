A layout puts each control where it's found and nothing where it gets in the way. Controls for one thing sit together, the rest sit apart, a bar takes the fewest rows it can, and nothing sits over what a person is watching unless the screen has no room left. Spacing and control sizes have [their own page](/foundations/spacing), and so do [touch targets](/foundations/spacing#touch-targets).

## Grouping

Things near each other read as one group, and things inside one boundary read as one group even more strongly. So a row holds its controls in groups, by what they act on, and the space between groups is clearly bigger than the space inside one.

| Between | Gap |
| --- | --- |
| Controls in one group | `--gw-space-2` (8px) |
| Groups in one row, with a separator | `--gw-space-3` (12px) each side of the line |
| Groups in one row, without one | `--gw-space-6` (24px) |
| Rows of controls | `--gw-space-2` (8px), or more where they act on different things |

- **A separator** is a 1px line in `--gw-color-border-quiet`, no taller than the controls beside it: a [divider](/components/layout#divider). It goes between two groups that act on different things, such as a camera's Home button and its presets, never between every control.
- **One group, one boundary.** A set of controls that only means something together, such as zoom out, the zoom level and zoom in, is one [control group](/components/actions#control-group), whose sections draw their own separators.
- **Sharing a row is the exception.** Controls for different things share a row only when there's no better place for them. Each group otherwise goes beside what it acts on.

## Fewest rows, then overflow

A bar of controls takes the fewest rows that fit. When it runs short of room it tightens first, in this order, and only takes another row once there's nothing left to tighten:

1. **Labels go.** Buttons with an icon show only the icon (`labels="auto"`), keeping their name for assistive technology.
2. **Seldom-used controls fold into More.** A More menu at the end of the bar lists them, in order. Each item mirrors its control's label, icon, disabled and pressed state, and choosing one presses the control, or follows it when it's a link. A [toggle](/components/actions#segmented-control) does the same with its own options through `overflow`.
3. **Controls shrink to their button.** A control with more than one part, such as [volume](/components/inputs#volume), becomes the button that opens it.
4. **Then two rows**, split between groups, never inside one, and as even as they can be. Rows never wrap unevenly, with one control left alone on a line.
5. **Then the phone layout.**

The order matters: a control in a More menu is one press further away, a second row takes space from the content, and the phone layout changes where everything is. So a bar gives up the least first.

To measure, each control has two widths: the least it can be, as its `min-inline-size` and `minInlineWidth`, and the width it takes with everything showing, as `inlineWidth`. `minInlineWidth` is there to read where a page's own styles, such as `* { min-width: 0 }`, override the `min-inline-size`. A bar adds them up to decide what fits, rather than waiting for something to overflow.

A [control bar](/components/actions#control-bar) does all of this for a bar in three groups, start, middle and end, with controls marked `data-overflow` folding into its More.

## Sizes that change the layout

Each threshold has a name, so every app changes at the same size. `@timmo001/groundwork-ui` exports each as a media query, for `matchMedia` or an app's own styles.

| Name | When | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Labels hide (`labelsHiddenQuery`) | Under 40rem (640px) wide, with `labels="auto"` | Buttons with an icon show only it |
| Sheet below (`sheetQuery()`) | Under 600px wide or 500px tall | Popovers, menus and dialogs open as [bottom sheets](/components/overlays#bottom-sheets-on-phones) |
| Phone on its side (`sidewaysQuery`) | `(orientation: landscape) and (height < 500px)` | Controls go over the content, gaps get smaller, and the edges are kept clear |

A component that changes with the room it has, not the window, uses a container query on itself, so it works the same in a sidebar or a dialog as on a page.

## A phone on its side

A phone turned sideways has a short, wide screen, so the content gets all of it:

- **Controls over the content.** A player's controls sit over its picture, as [Controls over a picture](/behavior#controls-over-a-picture) sets out, rather than taking rows from it.
- **Smaller gaps.** Gaps drop to `--gw-space-2`, and rows of controls to one where they can.
- **Clear of the edges.** Nothing that can be pressed goes in the rounded corners or the middle of a side, where the camera cutout sits. Keep an inline margin of `--gw-safe-inline` and a bottom margin of `--gw-safe-bottom`.

| Token | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `--gw-safe-inline` | `max(1.5rem, env(safe-area-inset-left), env(safe-area-inset-right))` |
| `--gw-safe-bottom` | `max(var(--gw-space-2), env(safe-area-inset-bottom))` |

The safe area insets are only there when the page asks to draw under the cutout and the rounded corners. Add `viewport-fit=cover` to the viewport `<meta>`, and keep everything that can be pressed inside the tokens' margins:

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, viewport-fit=cover" />
```

## Media fills its box

A picture or a video takes all of its box it can, and shows all of itself wherever its edge may matter.

- **Give the box the media's shape** wherever the layout allows, with `aspect-ratio`, so all of it shows, none of it is cut off and there are no bands around it.
- **Where the layout sets the shape**, such as a grid of tiles or full screen, it shows all of itself (`object-fit: contain`), with bands where the shapes differ. Something at the edge of the frame may be what matters: a person at the edge of a camera's view, or the end of a line in a document.
- **Covering is a choice, not the rule.** A tile that's only a preview, where nothing at its edge matters, such as a photo in an album, may cover its box (`object-fit: cover`) and lose its edges. An app can offer it as a setting, and zooming in may fill the box.
- **A stream sent squeezed**, such as a 16:9 picture in a 4:3 frame, is stretched back to its own shape (`object-fit: fill` in a box of the real ratio), never shown squeezed.

## Filling the window

A view that fills the window, such as a player or a map, takes the height under the app bar and any banners, and no more, so the page never scrolls behind it. Size it in `dvh`, which follows the browser's own bars on a phone, less what sits above it:

```css
.player {
  block-size: calc(100dvh - var(--gw-fill-top, 0px));
}
```

`fillWindow(player)` from `@timmo001/groundwork-ui` keeps `--gw-fill-top` at the view's own top edge, measuring again whenever what's above it changes, such as a banner appearing, and after full screen.
